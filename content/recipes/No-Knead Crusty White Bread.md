Rustic no-knead loaf that lives in the fridge and gets baked off in small batches over the course of a week. Big-batch dough, single mixing bowl, no kneading, crackly crust from a splash of water in the oven. Adapted from [King Arthur Baking's No-Knead Crusty White Bread](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/no-knead-crusty-white-bread-recipe); see the original for the full write-up.

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Yield | 3 to 4 loaves from one batch of dough |
| Active time | ~5 minutes to mix |
| Total time | 5+ hours (2 hr rise, 2+ hr chill, 1 hr shape/proof, 30 min bake) |
| Dough shelf life | Up to 7 days in the fridge |

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 900 g | 7½ cups |
| Lukewarm water | 680 g | 3 cups |
| Table salt | 18 g | 1 tbsp |
| Instant or active dry yeast | 14 g | 1½ tbsp |

## Method

### Mix the dough

Combine the flour, water, salt, and yeast in a large bowl or 6-quart lidded bucket. Stir until you have a sticky, shaggy dough with no dry pockets. No kneading. Cover loosely.

### First rise and chill

Let the dough rise at room temperature for 2 hours; it will roughly double and get bubbly on top. Move it to the fridge for at least 2 more hours, and up to 7 days. Cold-holding is where the flavor develops. It also firms the dough so it is easier to portion.

### Portion and shape

Pull off ¼ to ⅓ of the dough (400 to 540 g by weight for a standard loaf) onto a floured surface. Shape it into a rough ball by tucking the edges under itself a few times. Set the ball on a parchment-lined or greased baking sheet, dust with flour, and cover loosely.

### Second rise

Rest at room temperature for about 60 minutes. It will spread and swell rather than dramatically double.

### Preheat setup

While the loaf is rising, heat the oven to 450°F. If you use a baking stone, put it on the middle rack. Put a shallow metal pan (an old sheet pan works) on the lowest rack. This pan is what generates the steam.

### Score and bake

Right before baking, slash the top of the loaf 2 or 3 times about ½ inch deep with a sharp knife or lame. Slide the loaf into the oven, then pour 1 cup of hot water into the shallow pan below. Close the door quickly to trap the steam.

Bake 25 to 35 minutes, until deeply golden and hollow-sounding on the bottom when tapped.

### Cool

Transfer to a wire rack and let it cool at least 20 minutes before slicing. Cutting too early makes the crumb gummy.

## Notes

- **Weighing beats measuring.** Bread hydration is picky; a scale is worth using here.
- **Steam matters.** The hot water in the bottom pan is what gives the crust its crackle. Don't skip it.
- **Dough left in the fridge past 4-5 days** gets a more sourdough-like tang. Not bad, just different. After a week, start a fresh batch.
- **Score direction changes the look.** Parallel slashes for a baguette feel, a single deep cut for a boule, a cross for a country loaf.

Complete reference for primary ferment, flavoring, water, and grain care.

## Primary Ferment

| Parameter | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Water | 1 quart (4 cups) non-chlorinated water per batch |
| Sugar | 1/4 cup organic cane sugar per quart (ratio: ~1 tbsp sugar per cup of water) |
| Grains | 3-4 tablespoons per quart to start |
| Dried fruit | 1-2 dried figs (or substitute) per quart, unsulphured only |
| Lemon | 1 small slice per quart |
| Duration | 24-48 hours at room temperature (68-78°F ideal) |
| Cover | Cloth or coffee filter secured with a rubber band (not sealed) |
| Done when | Mildly sweet, not syrupy; small bubbles visible |

> [!note] Scaling up
> As grains multiply, scale proportionally. 6 cups water with ~1/3 cup sugar for a larger grain count. Too much water relative to grains equals slow and sweet. Too little equals sour and stressed culture.

## Secondary Ferment (Flavoring and Carbonation)

Strain out grains first. Grains never go into the second ferment. Seal in flip-top (Grolsch-style) bottles.

| Parameter | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Duration | 24-48 hours sealed at room temperature |
| Burping | Crack seal once daily to release excess pressure |
| Electrolytes | Add salt at this stage, not the primary ferment. 1/8 tsp sea salt or pink Himalayan per quart. Optional: a couple tbsp coconut water for potassium and magnesium. |
| When done | Strain fruit chunks, refrigerate, serve cold |

## Flavor Recipes (Per Quart)

| Flavor | Ingredients | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Green Tea Peach | 8 oz strong brewed green tea (cooled) + 1/2 diced ripe peach or 2-3 tbsp peach puree | Green tea tannins can slow carbonation; lean toward full 48 hrs. Frozen peach works fine. Optional: 1 tsp raw honey or splash of peach juice for more punch. |
| Lime | Juice of 1 lime + a few strips of zest | More acidic; may carbonate faster. Check at 24 hrs. Pairs well with 1-2 coin-sized slices of fresh ginger. |
| Ginger Lime | Juice of 1 lime + zest strips + 1-2 slices fresh ginger | Carbonates aggressively. Burp diligently. |

## Citrus Rules

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Juice (freshly squeezed) | Pith (white part), causes bitterness that worsens over time |
| Zest strips (aromatic oils, bright flavor) | Whole citrus with pith intact |

| Citrus Type | Sweetness | Pressure Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lemon / Lime | Low (mostly acid) | Standard carbonation pace |
| Orange / Tangerine / Blood Orange | High (brings own sugar) | Builds pressure faster; check and burp at 24 hrs |
| Grapefruit | Low-medium | Use half a grapefruit per quart max. Naringin in the juice can turn bitter even without pith. |

## Dried Fruit for Primary Ferment

Used in the primary ferment to replenish minerals for the grains. Must be unsulphured (no sulphur dioxide).

| Fruit | Mineral Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Figs (Turkish) **Best** | Highest, calcium, magnesium | Gold standard. 1-2 per quart. |
| Dried Apricots | High, potassium, iron | Imparts less flavor than figs; cleaner base for second ferment. |
| Medjool Dates | High | Adds caramel sweetness that carries into second ferment. Works with some flavors, muddies others. |
| Raisins | Lowest of the group | Most commonly recommended online but weakest mineral profile. Fine in a pinch. |

> [!warning] Critical
> Always check the ingredient list. It should be just the fruit. Sulphur dioxide (used as a preservative) will harm your grains. Organic and health food store options are the safest bet.

## Water Guidelines

| Water Type | Suitability |
|---|---|
| Berkey-filtered | Excellent. Removes chlorine, chloramine, and fluoride (with PF-2 filters). May strip some minerals; the dried fruit in the primary ferment compensates. |
| Carbon-filtered (Brita, fridge) | Good. Removes chlorine and chloramine. |
| Tap, sat out 24 hrs | OK for free chlorine only. Does not remove chloramine. Check your utility's water report. |
| Spring water | Good but not required. Natural mineral content is a plus. |
| Distilled / RO | Worst choice. Stripped of all minerals. If used, add trace mineral drops or a pinch of mineral salt. |

## Grain Storage

| Method | Duration | Process | Revival |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fridge in sugar water | 2-3 weeks per batch of sugar water | Place grains in fresh sugar water (same 1/4 cup per quart ratio), refrigerate. Swap sugar water every 2-3 weeks for longer storage. | Strain, bring to room temp, start a fresh ferment. First batch may take ~72 hrs and may taste off; discard and run a second batch. |
| Dehydrated | Months | Rinse grains, spread on parchment paper, air dry at room temp for 3-5 days until hard and yellowish. Store in sealed bag or jar in fridge. | Rehydrate over ~1 week with several batches of sugar water. Discard all revival batches. |

## General Tips

| Topic | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Metal contact | Avoid prolonged metal contact with grains. Use plastic strainers and wooden or plastic spoons. |
| Temperature | 68-78°F is the sweet spot. Cooler temps slow fermentation; warmer speeds it up. |
| Grain growth | Healthy grains multiply within a few weeks. Split and scale up or share extras. |
| Sluggish grains | If grains slow down or stop growing, try adding a few drops of trace mineral drops or a tiny pinch of baking soda to the primary ferment. |
| Starting out | Begin with single quart batches while dialing in your process. |

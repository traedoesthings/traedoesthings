Baker's percentages for common breads at a glance. Everything is expressed as a percentage of the total flour weight, which is always 100 percent. This is the same math professional bakers use to scale and compare formulas.

## The Formula

For any bread:

```
Hydration % = (water weight / flour weight) × 100
Salt %      = (salt weight / flour weight) × 100
Yeast %     = (yeast weight / flour weight) × 100
```

Example: 500 g flour, 350 g water, 10 g salt, 5 g instant yeast → 70% hydration, 2% salt, 1% yeast.

## Common Breads by Hydration

| Bread | Hydration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bagel | 55 to 60% | Stiff, tight crumb, holds the boil |
| Pretzel | 55 to 60% | Similar to bagel, needs firm shape |
| Pizza (Neapolitan) | 60 to 65% | Cold ferment friendly, thin crust |
| Sandwich loaf (pan) | 62 to 68% | Soft crumb, enriched often |
| Baguette | 65 to 72% | Open crumb, crackly crust |
| Country loaf / boule | 70 to 78% | Standard sourdough range |
| Ciabatta | 75 to 85% | Wet, sticky, big holes |
| Focaccia | 75 to 90% | Very wet, dimpled, olive oil finish |
| No-knead crusty loaf | 75 to 80% | Long ferment does the work |
| High-hydration sourdough | 80 to 90% | Open crumb, advanced handling |
| Whole wheat / rye | +5 to 10% | These flours absorb more; add water |

## Salt

| Salt Level | Ratio | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 1.5% | Under-seasoned, faster ferment |
| Standard | 1.8 to 2.0% | Target for most breads |
| High | 2.2 to 2.5% | Pretzels, focaccia, enriched breads |
| Too much | 3%+ | Slows or stops fermentation |

Salt slows yeast, so more salt equals a longer proof. If a recipe skips salt entirely, your dough will ferment fast, taste flat, and have weak gluten structure.

## Yeast

| Yeast Type | Standard Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Instant yeast | 0.5 to 1.0% | Mix directly with flour |
| Active dry yeast | 0.7 to 1.3% | Bloom in warm water first (or use 25% more than instant) |
| Fresh cake yeast | 1.5 to 3.0% | Use 3x the instant amount |
| Sourdough starter | 15 to 25% | Levain built at 100% hydration by default |

For an overnight cold retard, cut instant yeast to 0.3 to 0.5%. Long ferments build flavor; less yeast slows the rise so the timing works.

## Enrichments (Percentages of Flour Weight)

| Ingredient | Typical Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | 2 to 10% | Softer crumb, browner crust; 10%+ slows yeast |
| Butter or oil | 3 to 15% | Softer texture, tighter crumb |
| Egg | 10 to 25% | Richer, tender, brioche territory |
| Milk (or milk powder) | 5 to 15% | Softer crumb, brown crust |
| Honey / malt | 1 to 5% | Flavor, crust color, yeast food |

Brioche is where enrichments run wild: 50%+ butter and 25%+ egg are normal for the richest versions.

## A Full Formula, Written Out

Standard country boule at 74% hydration:

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Grams (500 g flour) |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 100% | 500 |
| Water | 74% | 370 |
| Levain (100% hydration) | 20% | 100 |
| Salt | 2% | 10 |

Total dough weight: 980 g.

To scale up or down, keep the percentages fixed and change only the flour weight; everything else scales with it.

## Quick Conversions

| I have | Multiply flour by | To get |
|---|---|---|
| Hydration % | 0.01 × flour g | Water grams |
| Salt % | 0.01 × flour g | Salt grams |
| 500 g flour, 75% hydration | 500 × 0.75 | 375 g water |
| 1000 g flour, 2% salt | 1000 × 0.02 | 20 g salt |

## Rules of Thumb

- **Higher hydration equals more open crumb** and a softer, wetter dough that is harder to shape. Practice at 70% before jumping to 80%.
- **Whole grains absorb more water.** Bump hydration 5 to 10% when substituting whole wheat, rye, or spelt for AP or bread flour.
- **Colder ambient temperature slows fermentation** proportionally. Every 15°F drop roughly doubles proof time.
- **The dough tells you.** Percentages are a starting point. Flour absorption varies by brand, humidity, and age, so hold back 5% of the water on a new recipe and add if the dough feels dry.

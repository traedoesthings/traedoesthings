A one-page cheat sheet for the settings and rules of thumb that come up every time you pick up a camera. Start with the exposure triangle, then use the scenario table when you are out shooting.

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Exposure triangle | Aperture, shutter speed, ISO |
| Default mode | Aperture priority (A or Av) with Auto ISO |
| Safe handheld shutter | 1 divided by your focal length, or faster |
| Best light | Golden hour and open shade |

## The Exposure Triangle

| Setting | Controls | Lower number or slower | Higher number or faster |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aperture (f-stop) | Depth of field and light | Wide open (f/1.8): blurry background, more light | Stopped down (f/11): more in focus, less light |
| Shutter speed | Motion and light | 1/30s: blur, more light | 1/1000s: frozen action, less light |
| ISO | Sensor sensitivity | ISO 100: clean, needs light | ISO 6400: noisy, works in the dark |

Each full stop doubles or halves the light. Trade one setting for another to keep the same exposure: open the aperture one stop and you can speed up the shutter one stop.

## Starting Settings by Scenario

| Scenario | Mode | Aperture | Shutter | ISO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait | Aperture priority | f/1.8 to f/2.8 | 1/200s or faster | 100 to 400 |
| Landscape | Aperture priority, tripod | f/8 to f/11 | Whatever it needs | 100 |
| Street | Aperture priority | f/5.6 to f/8 | 1/250s or faster | Auto |
| Sports and wildlife | Shutter priority or manual | Widest available | 1/1000s or faster | Auto |
| Low light handheld | Aperture priority | Widest available | 1/60s or faster | 1600 to 6400 |
| Night sky | Manual, tripod | Widest available | 15 to 20s | 1600 to 3200 |
| Macro | Aperture priority | f/8 to f/16 | 1/125s or faster | 200 to 400 |
| Moving water (silky) | Shutter priority, tripod | f/11 or higher | 0.5 to 2s | 100 |

## Composition Rules of Thumb

- **Rule of thirds:** place the subject or horizon on a gridline or intersection instead of dead center.
- **Leading lines:** roads, fences, and rivers pull the eye toward the subject.
- **Framing:** use doorways, branches, or windows to wrap the subject.
- **Negative space:** leave room around the subject so it can breathe.
- **Eye level and eyes in focus:** for people and animals, focus on the nearest eye.
- **Simplify the background:** move a few steps and a pole stops growing out of someone's head.

## Light

| Light | When | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Golden hour | First and last hour of sun | Warm, soft, long shadows |
| Blue hour | Just before sunrise or after sunset | Cool, even, moody |
| Open shade | Any sunny day | Soft and flattering for portraits |
| Overcast | Cloudy days | Even light, good for color and detail |
| Midday sun | Around noon | Harsh, so look for shade or shoot backlit |

## Focus and Metering

- **Single point AF (AF-S):** still subjects.
- **Continuous AF (AF-C):** anything that moves.
- **Back button focus:** separates focusing from the shutter so you can lock focus and recompose.
- **Evaluative or matrix metering:** a good default.
- **Spot metering:** backlit subjects and high contrast scenes.
- **Exposure compensation:** dial +1 for snow and bright beaches, minus 1 for dark scenes the camera is over-brightening.

## File and Workflow Basics

- Shoot RAW when you plan to edit, since it holds far more highlight and shadow detail than JPEG.
- Expose to protect the highlights. Shadows can be lifted in editing, blown highlights cannot be recovered.
- Check the histogram instead of trusting the screen brightness.
- Keep white balance on Auto for RAW, or set it to match the light for JPEG.

## Notes

- Handheld shutter speed rule: 1 divided by focal length. A 50mm lens wants 1/50s or faster, a 200mm lens wants 1/200s or faster. Image stabilization buys you a few extra stops, moving subjects do not care about it.
- Modern cameras handle high ISO well. A sharp, noisy photo beats a clean, blurry one every time.
- On a crop sensor, multiply the focal length by about 1.5 for the equivalent field of view.
- Wide apertures on close subjects have razor thin focus. If eyes look soft, stop down a notch.
- Clean the front of the lens before you shoot. Most mystery haze is a fingerprint.

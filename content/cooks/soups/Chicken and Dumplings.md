A creamy, one-pot chicken and dumplings built on bone-in thighs, a tomato paste and flour base, and fluffy green onion dumplings steamed right on top. It is hearty, forgiving, and easy to make soupier or thicker depending on your mood.

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Pot | Dutch oven |
| Simmer | 20 minutes covered |
| Dumplings | 10 minutes steaming |
| Heat | Medium |

## Ingredients

### Soup

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone in and skinless | 5 |
| Carrots, diced | 2 |
| Yellow onion, diced | 1 |
| Celery stalks, diced | 4 |
| Garlic cloves, diced | 3 |
| Tomato paste | 2.5 tbsp |
| All-purpose flour | 2.5 tbsp |
| Chicken stock | 4 cups |
| Heavy cream | 1 cup |
| Water | 1/2 cup |
| Spinach | 1 whole bunch |
| Butter | As needed (about 2 tbsp) |
| Olive oil | As needed |
| Dried thyme | To taste |
| Bay leaves | 2 |
| Shredded Parmesan (optional) | To taste |

### Seasonings

No measurements here. Use as much or as little as you like.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Italian seasoning | To taste |
| Onion powder | To taste |
| Garlic powder | To taste |
| Lemon pepper seasoning (salt free) | To taste |
| Paprika | To taste |
| Salt | To taste |
| Black pepper | To taste |

### Dumplings

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 1 cup |
| Baking powder | 1 tsp |
| Green onion, chopped | To taste |
| Milk | 1/2 cup |
| Black pepper | To taste |
| Salt | To taste |

## Method

1. Season the chicken thighs on all sides with the seasonings above.
2. Brown the chicken in a Dutch oven with a tbsp of butter over medium heat, 3 to 4 minutes per side.
3. Pull the chicken once it has color and a good crust on each side and set it aside.
4. Add the diced onion, celery, and carrots to the pot with another tbsp of butter.
5. Add a pinch of salt. Just a tiny one.
6. Cook until the veggies start to look soft, 6 to 7 minutes over medium heat.
7. Stir in the diced garlic and tomato paste and cook for about 3 minutes.
8. Add the 2.5 tbsp of flour and mix it into the veggies. This is what thickens the soup. Cook for 2 minutes.
9. Gradually add the chicken stock, stirring with a wooden spoon. Stir and pour, stir and pour, a few times, until it is all combined and the bottom of the pot is scraped clean. Bring to a simmer.
10. Return the browned chicken to the pot, add the dried thyme and 2 bay leaves, and stir. Cover and cook for 20 minutes.
11. Take the chicken out and put it in a bowl. Let it rest before you touch it. It is much hotter than it looks. Once it has cooled a bit, shred it.
12. Put the shredded chicken back in the pot.
13. Stir in the heavy cream, then adjust the seasoning in the broth. Salt, pepper, and paprika, to your liking. If the broth looks too thick, add the half cup of water. Add more if you want it soupier.
14. Cover the pot and go make the dumplings.
15. Dumplings: combine the 1 cup flour, baking powder, green onion, milk, pepper, and salt. Mix until just combined.
16. Roll the batter into small balls with your hands and lightly drop them into the soup. Cover the pot and let them steam for 10 minutes.
17. Add the spinach and cook for 3 minutes. Smaller dumplings make it easier to tuck the spinach in around them.
18. Top with shredded Parmesan if you like.

## Notes

- **Dumpling flavor:** the dumplings were good but could use more seasoning. Next time, try adding a splash of chicken broth or a little bouillon to the batter.
- **Chives:** green onion stands in for chives here and works fine.
- **Flour:** all-purpose worked, but a different flour might be worth a try.
- **Spinach timing:** if you have a better time to add the spinach, it may be easier before the dumplings go in. Worth experimenting with.
- **Thickness:** the broth thins out a lot with the water and cream, so start with less water if you like it thick.
- **Resting the chicken:** let it sit a few minutes before shredding.

Verdict: 10/10, would make again.

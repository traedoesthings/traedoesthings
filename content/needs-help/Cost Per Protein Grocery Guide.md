A quick reference for grocery shopping when you want protein without overpaying for it. Every food here is priced per 25 grams of protein, which is about one serving of meat, so you can compare a pound of chicken to a tub of whey to a bag of lentils on equal footing. Prices are tuned for the Charlottesville, Virginia area as of November 2026.

## How to Use It

**Shop the top of the list first.** Lentils, beans, eggs, milk, chicken, and pork chops all land under about \$1.10 per 25g. Building most of your week around those keeps the bill low.

**Watch the calories if you are cutting.** Chicken breast, canned tuna, whey, and shrimp give you the most protein for the fewest calories. Peanut butter and beans are cheap, but they bring a lot of carbs or fat with the protein, so they work better as a supporting player than the main source.

**Know where the price jumps.** Beef and salmon cost two to three times as much per gram of protein as chicken. They are great for variety, just not for everyday protein.

**Check the label for the final word.** Ground meats change a lot with fat percentage, and packaged foods like yogurt and bars vary by brand. Use this chart to decide where to look, then confirm the numbers on the shelf tag.

## Notes

- Cost per 25g is the package price divided by total grams of protein in the package, times 25.
- Meat is figured on raw weight, and beans and lentils on dry weight.
- Calorie figures are rough typical values and will vary by brand and cut.
- BLS publishes prices by Census region, not by city, so Charlottesville numbers lean on South region averages. Chicken breast also reflects local store listings, which ran from about \$3.59 to \$4.99 per pound.
- Foods marked as estimates (lentils, beans, peanut butter, tuna, whey, tofu, ground turkey, yogurt, cottage cheese, shrimp, deli turkey, protein bars, salmon, chicken thighs, sirloin) are typical supermarket prices rather than published averages. Swap in what you see at your store.
